What is this?

Every Chinese region has stories tourists never hear — the warrior dance born from a novel, the village that escorts the dead home, the ghost city on the river. These are the tales behind the festivals, the dances, and the places we film. Told in English, straight from local folklore.

Fengdu Ghost City, China — the Town Built for the Dead

On a mountain above the Yangtze in China, a whole city was built for the dead — bridges only the virtuous cross, demon judges, and a tower where the dead look home one last time. Visit it today.

Yingge Dance, China — the 'Warrior Dance' That Went Viral

In Chaoshan, China, painted warriors swing batons in a 400-year-old 'warrior dance' the internet can't stop watching.

Zhong Kui, China — the Bearded God Who Eats Ghosts for Breakfast

In China, families hang a painting of Zhong Kui to ward off evil — the scowling god who failed the imperial exam, died in disgrace, and returned to devour demons.

Nuo Opera, China — 3,000-Year-Old Play Where Masks Are Gods"

In remote villages of China, actors don masks and become the gods. Nuo opera is a 3,000-year-old exorcism disguised as theatre."
