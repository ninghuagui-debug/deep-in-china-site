Guilin-Li-River

桂林 · 漓江

Deep in China • Travel Guide

Last updated: August 2026

Plenty of people come to Guilin for the Li River, but most do the standard cruise down a crowded stretch and miss the part that actually made the postcard famous. Take the walk along the river between Xingping and Yangshuo instead — that's where the 20 yuan banknote scenery is shot, and it's far better on foot than from a tour boat deck.

The 20 Yuan Note Viewpoint, Broken Down

If you've held a 20 yuan bill in China, you've already seen this stretch of the Li River without knowing it. The scene on the back is the karst peak cluster near Xingping, in Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region. The landmark peak is called Bi Lian Feng (Green Lotus Peak), and the viewpoint sits right beside the riverbank where the bill's composition lines up almost exactly with what you see with your own eyes.

  • Where: Xingping (兴坪), about 25 km upriver from Yangshuo, on the Guilin–Yangshuo section.
  • How to get the exact frame: Stand by the low stone wall near the river, hold the 20 yuan note up, and the peaks behind it will match. It's gimmicky, but the match is genuinely good.
  • Tip: Come early morning or late afternoon. Midday light flattens the reflection on the water.

First-Person Notes From an Afternoon on the River

I walked the bank between Xingping and the Nine Horses Mural Hill on a weekday in late summer. The path is a mix of gravel, low stone steps, and bamboo groves. No big tour groups that far down the bank — just a few fishermen, a water buffalo doing nothing, and the occasional bamboo raft drifting past loaded with a handful of tourists and one bored-looking guide.

The water was green and still enough to mirror the peaks. Every few hundred meters the river bends and the landscape reshuffles — a peak that looked dominant ten minutes ago slides behind another one. That's the part no cruise photo can give you: the way the same hills keep rearranging themselves as you move.

There are small stalls along the way selling grilled fish on sticks and cold drinks. A raft operator called out a price for a short ride — we bargained a bit and took it for a quick loop, then walked the rest. Half the fun is that nothing is scheduled. You drift, you stop when something looks good, you keep going.

Xingping: The Town Most Tourists Skip

Most Guilin itineraries anchor on Li River cruises from Guilin city and end at Yangshuo's West Street, which is fine but busy. Xingping sits in between and almost nobody stops there overnight. It's a small riverside town with a couple of old streets, rice noodle shops, and guesthouses with views over the same peaks the banknote shows.

Spending one night in Xingping is the single smartest move on this route. You get the banknote viewpoint at sunrise — empty — and a quiet evening by the river before Yangshuo gets loud.

How to Work This Into a Guilin–Yangshuo Trip

A workable two-day plan, without chasing every listed attraction:

  • Day 1: Guilin city → bus or train to Xingping (about 1.5–2 hours). Settle in, walk the bank, catch the 20 yuan viewpoint late afternoon.
  • Day 2: Early walk to Nine Horses Mural Hill and back, then a short drive to Yangshuo. If you want the classic raft-with-motor experience, take one of the short Xingping–Yangshuo river segments rather than the full Guilin-to-Yangshuo cruise.

If you've got more days, Yangshuo's back roads are worth a bike ride — the karst fields around the town are the real draw, and quieter than the main street. That's a better "things to do in Yangshuo off the beaten path" answer than any single attraction.

A Few Practical Things

  • Getting there: Trains and buses run between Guilin and Xingping; from Xingping to Yangshuo it's a short ride. Everything is doable in a day trip, but overnight in Xingping makes it better.
  • What to bring: Sunscreen, water, cash for the stalls (small vendors often don't take cards), a camera — and a 20 yuan note for the photo trick.
  • Best season: Dry months, roughly October to April, give clearer views. Summer is green but humid, with more chance of rain haze.

Bottom Line

The Guilin Li River cruise is the famous option, but the better one is slower: base yourself in Xingping, walk the bank for the 20 yuan note viewpoint, and let the peaks do the talking. No boat schedule, no photo queue of strangers — just the landscape that's been on China's money for years, seen from the bank instead of the brochure.
